Lecture 1: The Economy as a Whole

Growth, Prices, Employment, and Aggregate Foundations

1. Definition & Historical Context

Scarcity Core Principle

Economics studies how society uses scarce resources to produce valuable goods/services and distribute them among people.
Scarcity = Limited Resources vs. Unlimited Wants.

Key Historical Pillars

1776 – Adam Smith (Wealth of Nations): Promoted free markets, self-interest, competition, and minimal government intervention.

1936 – J. M. Keynes (General Theory): Following the Great Depression, argued government spending & policy interventions are essential to manage economic downturns.

2. Microeconomics vs. Macroeconomics

Basis Microeconomics Macroeconomics
Scope Individual consumers, firms, specific markets The economy as a whole
Main Focus Price determination, consumer choices, firm costs National income, inflation, unemployment, growth
Key Variables Demand, supply, elasticity, profit margins GDP, Inflation rate, Interest rates, Money supply
Policy Focus Individual market regulation, single tax rates Fiscal policy (spending/taxes), Monetary policy
Example A rise in coffee bean prices changes coffee shop sales Government expenditure during recession boosts national employment
Micro-Macro Linkage: Macro outcomes result from millions of micro-level decisions by households and firms. In turn, macro policies (interest rates, taxes) shape individual micro behavior.

3. Macroeconomic Variables & Interrelation Channels

GDP & Growth Total output produced & year-over-year % growth.
Inflation Rate Rate at which the general price level rises.
Unemployment Rate % of willing & able labor force without jobs.
Interest Rates Cost of borrowing / return on saving.
Fiscal Deficit & Debt Govt expenditure gap & cumulative borrowing.
Balance of Payments Complete record of international trade & capital flows.

Core Relationship Channels:

Growth Channel Consumption + Investment ↑ → Aggregate Demand ↑ → Output (GDP) ↑ → Employment ↑
Inflation Channel Money Supply ↑ → Demand ↑ → Inflation ↑ → Central Bank raises Interest Rates → Spending cools
Okun's Law (Trade-off) Inverse relationship: Aggregate Output (GDP) ↑ → Unemployment Rate ↓

4. Stock vs. Flow Variables

Stock Variable Point in Time

Measured at a specific instant; contains no time dimension.

Analogy: Water level in a bathtub at 10:00 AM (Photo snapshot).

Examples: Wealth on March 31, Population on Jan 1, Money Supply today, National Debt.

Flow Variable Over a Duration

Measured over a specified time period (e.g. per month or year).

Analogy: Rate of water flowing from the faucet into the tub (Video over time).

Examples: Annual GDP, Monthly Salary, Quarterly National Income, Fiscal Deficit per year.

N.B. Income is a FLOW variable; Wealth is a STOCK variable.

5. Short Run vs. Long Run Perspectives

Microeconomics Context

Short Run: At least one Factor of Production (FOP) is fixed (e.g., factory size/machines fixed, labor variable).

Long Run: All FOPs are fully variable. Firms can alter plant sizes or exit/enter market.

Macroeconomics Context

Short Run (Sticky Prices): Prices and wages adjust slowly due to contracts. Aggregate demand dictates production fluctuations & business cycles.

Long Run (Flexible Prices): Prices/wages are fully flexible. Output depends on real supply factors (Capital, Labor, Technology, Institutions).

Lecture 2: Measuring GDP & National Income

Nominal/Real GDP, GDP Deflator, Inflation, Expenditure & Income Approaches

1. Definition of GDP & Core Principles

"GDP is the market value of all final goods and services produced within a country in a given period of time."

• Market Value: Evaluated using market prices reflecting consumer willingness to pay.

• Final Goods: Only final products included; avoids double counting intermediate components.

• Domestic: Production within geographic borders (regardless of owner nationality).

• Gross vs Net: Gross is BEFORE subtracting capital depreciation. Net = Gross - Depreciation.

Crucial Exam Note: What GDP Excludes!

  • Intermediate goods (already in final price)
  • Sale of used/second-hand goods
  • Financial transactions (stocks, bonds)
  • Government Transfer Payments (pensions, welfare)
  • Home-produced non-market goods
  • Illegal & unreported informal activities
  • Leisure time, environmental quality, income equity

2. Components of GDP (Expenditure Identity)

Expenditure Identity Formula Y = C + I + G + NX
Consumption (C) Household spending on goods/services. Excludes new home purchases.
Investment (I) Business capital equipment, inventory build, and new residential housing purchases.
Govt Purchases (G) Spending on goods/services by federal/local govt. Excludes transfer payments.
Net Exports (NX) Exports (X) minus Imports (M). Domestic spending on foreign goods subtracts out.

3. Nominal vs. Real GDP & Deflator Worked Problem

Nominal GDP Formula Nominal GDP = ∑ (Current Price × Current Qty)

Reflects changes in BOTH prices and quantities.

Real GDP Formula Real GDP = ∑ (Base Year Price × Current Qty)

Reflects changes ONLY in actual output quantities.

Exam Lecture Sample Data:

Good Year 1 Price (Base) Year 1 Quantity Year 2 Price Year 2 Quantity
Rice Tk. 50 1,000 kg Tk. 60 1,100 kg
Mobile Phones Tk. 20,000 100 units Tk. 22,000 120 units

Step-by-Step Calculation Breakdown:

Step 1: Nominal GDP Calculation

• Year 1 (Nominal): (50 × 1,000) + (20,000 × 100) = 50,000 + 2,000,000 = Tk. 2,050,000

• Year 2 (Nominal): (60 × 1,100) + (22,000 × 120) = 66,000 + 2,640,000 = Tk. 2,706,000

Step 2: Real GDP Calculation (Base Year = Year 1)

• Year 1 (Real): Equals Nominal GDP in base year = Tk. 2,050,000

• Year 2 (Real): (50 × 1,100) + (20,000 × 120) = 55,000 + 2,400,000 = Tk. 2,455,000

Step 3: GDP Deflator & Inflation Rate

GDP Deflator = (Nominal GDP / Real GDP) × 100

• Deflator Year 1 = (2,050,000 / 2,050,000) × 100 = 100

• Deflator Year 2 = (2,706,000 / 2,455,000) × 100 = 110.22

Inflation Rate = [(Deflator_Y2 - Deflator_Y1) / Deflator_Y1] × 100

• Inflation Rate Year 2 = [(110.22 - 100) / 100] × 100 = 10.22%

4. National Income Accounting Methods

Expenditure Approach GDP = C + I + G + (X - M)

Sums aggregate spending by households, businesses, government, and net foreign buyers.

Worked Example:

Given: C = 1,500,000; I = 600,000; G = 400,000; X = 350,000; M = 150,000

1. Net Exports (NX) = 350,000 - 150,000 = 200,000

2. GDP = 1,500,000 + 600,000 + 400,000 + 200,000

GDP = Tk. 2,700,000

Income Approach Factor Income + Taxes + Depr

Sums total income earned by factors of production (Labor, Land, Capital, Entrepreneurship).

Worked Example:

Given: Wages = 500, Rent = 100, Interest = 80, Profit = 120, Indirect Tax = 50, Depreciation = 30

GDP = 500 + 100 + 80 + 120 + 50 + 30

GDP = 880
